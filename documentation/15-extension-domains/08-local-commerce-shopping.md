# Local commerce & shopping

> Document part of documentation/15-extension-domains/ — guide to LocalMind developments. Date: 2026-06-29.

## 1. What we solve (problem & value)

### 1.1 The concrete problem

Neighborhood commerce — local shops, artisan workshops, district markets, producers of regional specialties — is the economic and cultural fabric of territories, yet it is also the segment most penalized by current digitalization. Three phenomena converge and produce cumulative damage:

1. **Digital invisibility.** Most small retailers do not have a website, or they have an incomplete Google Business Profile, outdated opening-hours data, no structured catalog. When a customer searches for "where to buy mountain-pasture cheese within 5 km of here" or "cobbler who repairs boots downtown," the results reward the large platforms and international marketplaces, not the shop 300 meters away. In Italy, online purchases will exceed 40 billion euros in 2026 (+6% year over year), but no local marketplace appears in the top 10: the value generated by the territory flows out to extraterritorial operators.

2. **Data fragmentation.** Information about a shop is scattered and inconsistent: hours on Google, prices on a paper flyer, product availability only "by asking in the store," reviews on three different platforms, market events published on a municipal Facebook page. No one holds together *shop → product → artisan → raw material → event → neighborhood → customer*. What is precisely missing are the **relationships** between these entities, which are what make intelligent discovery possible.

3. **Technological and cost asymmetry.** Existing solutions (SaaS marketplaces, cloud management systems, big-tech agentic commerce) impose commissions, data lock-in, the sending of commercial data to external servers, and visibility mediated by opaque algorithms. For an artisan this means giving up margin and losing control of the customer relationship. For a municipality or a tourist board (Pro Loco) it means being unable to offer a territory-promotion service without depending on an external supplier.

The 2026 evolution of e-commerce toward **agentic commerce** and **product knowledge graphs** (the "discovery before search," where a graph decides which products emerge in AI responses) makes the problem urgent: if local commerce is not represented in a structured and relational way, it will be literally absent from the responses of the AI assistants that customers will use to decide what to buy and where.

### 1.2 The LocalMind solution

LocalMind addresses the problem not by building "yet another marketplace," but by applying its **universal, weighted, AI-navigable knowledge graph engine** to the domain of local commerce. The difference is substantial:

- Instead of a flat product catalog, we model a **weighted graph** that connects shops, artisans, products, raw materials, merchandise categories, markets, events, neighborhoods, certifications, and customers. The graph captures *why* a product is relevant to a customer: proximity, seasonality, short supply chain, artisanal quality, complementarity with other purchases, belonging to a local tradition.
- The AI (GraphRAG) **navigates** this graph to answer complex questions and surface non-obvious connections ("which shops use flours from the local mill?", "compose a gift basket of only local specialties from my neighborhood under 40 €", "find an artisan who repairs this vintage object").
- Everything stays **local-first and self-hostable**: a municipality, a Commerce District, a network of shops, or a single association can host its own instance, with local AI (Ollama) by default. **Commercial data never leaves the infrastructure without explicit consent** — a decisive requirement for retailers who are protective of their sales data and for privacy compliance.

### 1.3 Value per stakeholder

| Stakeholder | Value generated |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Small retailer / artisan | Structured digital visibility at zero commission cost, data control, AI-driven discovery, relational storefront (not just a "listing") |
| Customer / consumer | Intelligent discovery of neighborhood commerce, AI answers to real questions ("what local specialty do I buy here?"), transparent supply chain |
| Municipality / Commerce District / Pro Loco | Self-hosted territory-promotion tool, living map of local commerce, aggregated data for commercial policies |
| Network of shops / specialty consortium | Collective promotion of the supply chain, cross-selling among complementary retailers, storytelling of tradition |
| Local community | Bottom-up contributions (reviews, reports, photos), "Wikipedia of local commerce" |

### 1.4 Why now and why different from competitors

The models analyzed (Google Knowledge Panel + AI Overview for local businesses, big-retailer product knowledge graphs, franchised proximity marketplaces like "Comuni a Domicilio," handmade platforms like Etsy) all have at least one of these limitations: they are **cloud-only**, **commission-based**, **proprietary over the data**, or **non-relational** (flat catalog). LocalMind is the only approach that combines: relational weighted graph + local AI + self-hosting + open source + bilingual + plugin extensibility. It does not compete on price or logistics, but on the **sovereignty of territorial data and the quality of discovery**.

## 3. Input requirements

This section defines in detail **which data the system must be able to acquire**, from whom, and with what quality, validation, and privacy constraints. It is the entry contract of the "Local commerce & shopping" domain.

### 3.1 Basic registry entities (shop / retailer)

For each commercial business, the system must be able to collect:

| Field | Requirement | Type / constraint | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| Business name / sign | Mandatory | String | Public sign + legal name |
| Activity type | Mandatory | Enum translated IT/EN | E.g. food, clothing, crafts, services, hardware… |
| VAT number / tax code | Optional (recommended) | Validated string | For disambiguation and verification; never publicly exposed by default |
| Full address | Mandatory | Street/number/ZIP/city structure | Geocodable |
| Geographic coordinates | Derived | lat/lon (decimal) | From address geocoding or manual input |
| Neighborhood / area / commercial street | Optional | Reference to Neighborhood node | Enables proximity queries |
| Contacts | At least one mandatory | Phone, email, website, social | Validated for format |
| Opening hours | Mandatory | Weekly structure + exceptions | Holidays, seasonal closures, shifts |
| Sales modes | Mandatory | Multiple enum IT/EN | In store, takeaway, local delivery, reservation, shipping |
| Languages spoken | Optional | List | Useful for tourism |
| Accessibility | Optional | Multiple enum IT/EN | Disabled access, parking, pet-friendly |
| Payment methods | Optional | Multiple enum IT/EN | Cash, cards, local vouchers |
| Description / history | Optional | Free text | Material for semantic embedding and storytelling |
| Photos / media | Optional | File (images) | Storefront, products, environment |
| Certifications / recognitions | Optional | References to Certification nodes | PDO, PGI, "Historic shop," Slow Food presidium… |

### 3.3 Markets and commercial events

- District markets and fairs: name, periodicity (weekly/monthly/annual cadence), days and hours, location (square/area), list of stalls/exhibitors present.
- Events: festivals, seasonal markets, "shopping white nights," tastings, artisan workshops, coordinated district sales: date(s), place, participating retailers, products/themes.

### 3.4 Community contributions

- Reviews and ratings (with score and free text), with an identified or pseudonymous author.
- Reports: new shop, incorrect data, closure, change of hours.
- Photos and media uploaded by users.
- Votes/upvotes on shops, products, reviews (feed the emergent ranking).
- Suggested tags and connections ("this product uses raw materials from…").

### 3.5 Validation and data-quality rules (input boundary)

Consistently with the project rules (validation at all boundaries, fail-fast, never trust external data):

- **Format validation:** VAT number (checksum), email, phone, URL, coordinates in range, non-negative prices, coherent hours (opening < closing).
- **Semantic validation:** category belonging to the taxonomy; allowed enums; references to existing nodes (no dangling edges).
- **Deduplication:** detection of duplicate retailers by similarity (name + address + VAT number) before node creation.
- **Verification status:** each node has a status (`draft` / `under review` / `verified` / `claimed by the retailer` / `reported`) that weighs on trust.
- **Data provenance:** each piece of data carries its source (retailer, community, external connector, import) and timestamp, for traceability and for weight calculation.
- **Privacy by design:** sensitive data (VAT number, private contacts, customer data) not publicly exposed by default; explicit consent for any synchronization toward external services or cloud LLM providers. Local AI by default.
- **i18n:** every enum label and every taxonomy available in IT and EN; free texts keep the original language with a language flag.

### 3.6 Acquisition modes

Input can arrive in four ways, in increasing order of automation: (1) **manual entry** via UI (retailer or curator); (2) **claiming and completion** of a pre-existing node by the retailer; (3) **bulk import** (CSV/JSON, e.g. a municipality's list of retailers); (4) **automatic connectors** (see §6) that generate nodes/edges from external sources. Every mode passes through the same validation rules (§3.5).

## 4. Activity flow (step-by-step)

We describe the main end-to-end flows. They are the basis for defining endpoints, domain services, and frontend components.

### 4.1 Flow A — Onboarding and mapping of the territory (deployer + curator)

1. **Instance installation** self-hosted (Docker compose: MySQL + Qdrant + Ollama). Selection of the "Local commerce & shopping" domain module from the plugin marketplace.
2. **Territory configuration:** definition of the area (municipality/neighborhoods), import of the base IT/EN merchandise taxonomy and of the relevant certifications.
3. **Initial seed:** bulk import of a list of retailers (municipal CSV) → validation pipeline (§3.5) → creation of `Shop` nodes in `draft` status, geocoding, deduplication.
4. **Enrichment via connectors:** execution of connectors (§6) to complete hours, public reviews, links → creation/update of edges.
5. **Semantic indexing:** descriptions and catalogs are chunked + embedded on Qdrant; the structural graph is populated on MySQL.
6. **Publication:** the curator promotes the nodes from `draft` to `verified`; the instance is navigable.

### 4.2 Flow B — The retailer claims and manages their storefront

1. The retailer finds their own listing (or creates it) and starts the **claim** with verification (code, domain email, or curator approval).
2. Once verified, they access a management area: complete the registry, **upload the product catalog** (manual, CSV, or connector from their own management/e-commerce system — see §6), set hours, photos, seasonality.
3. They define **supply-chain relationships**: connect their products to raw materials/producers ("I use flours from Mill X"), to other complementary shops, to events they participate in.
4. Every change passes through **validation** and generates a domain event (`CommerceNodeUpdated`) that triggers re-embedding and weight recalculation.
5. The retailer sees **analytics**: how many times their storefront emerged in AI responses, which queries found it, which connections the community has suggested.

### 4.3 Flow C — The customer discovers local commerce (consumer, GraphRAG)

1. The customer poses a **natural-language question** in the chat ("where do I find naturally leavened bread open now near Porta Nuova?") or uses the **graph search/navigation**.
2. The system combines: (a) **structural filters** on the graph (proximity, current opening hours, category, availability), (b) **semantic search** on Qdrant (intent "natural leavening, artisanal"), (c) **graph expansion** for weighted relationships (supply chain, complementarity, emergent quality).
3. GraphRAG selects the most relevant nodes/paths, the local AI **generates the answer citing the nodes** (shops/products) and the criteria ("open now, 400 m away, uses local flours, high community rating").
4. The customer explores the **interactive graph**: from the shop, expands to products, to events, to nearby complementary shops; filters by node/relationship type.
5. Actions: save, contact the retailer, add to a shopping itinerary, leave a review/vote (→ Flow D).

### 4.4 Flow D — Community contribution and emergent ranking

1. A user adds a review/vote/photo or reports a correction on a node.
2. The contribution enters `under review` status; it passes through **moderation** (automatic on rules + curation queue for doubtful cases).
3. Once accepted, the contribution **updates the weights** of the edges (e.g. perceived quality, popularity) and the node's trust.
4. The **emergent ranking** recalculates: shops/products with more positive signals and relevant relationships emerge more in AI responses and in navigation.
5. Controversial or reported contributions are escalated to the curator; an immutable audit trail is kept (new version, never in-place mutation).

### 4.5 Flow E — Collective promotion and cross-selling (district/consortium)

1. The district manager creates an **event** or a **campaign** (e.g. "Local Taste Week") connecting retailers and products.
2. The AI suggests **non-obvious connections** for cross-selling ("these 3 shops have complementary supply chains for a gift basket").
3. Themed **shopping itineraries** are generated on the graph (on foot, by neighborhood, by supply chain).
4. The campaign metrics (visibility, discoveries generated) return to the manager and the retailers.

### 4.6 Error handling along the flows

In every flow: fail-fast validation with clear, bilingual user messages; errors logged server-side with context; no silenced errors; transactional rollback on node/edge creation; retry queue for external connectors; graceful degradation of the AI response when the graph is sparse ("I found little data on this area, here is what I know").

## 5. Graph model (node types, relationship types, weighting criteria)

The domain reuses the **universal graph engine** (typed nodes + weighted edges on MySQL for the structure, Qdrant for semantics). Below are the types specific to the domain. The types are extendable via modular schema and all enums are translated IT/EN.

### 5.1 Node types

| Node type | Description | Key attributes |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| `Negozio` (Shop/Business) | Physical point of sale | sign, type, address, geo, hours, sales modes, verification status |
| `Artigiano` / `Produttore` (Artisan / Producer) | Person/workshop that produces | name, craft, techniques, territory |
| `Prodotto` (Product) | Good sold | name, price/range, availability, seasonality, attributes |
| `Servizio` (Service) | Service (repair, made-to-measure…) | name, price range, lead times |
| `CategoriaMerceologica` (Merchandise category) | Hierarchical taxonomy node | name IT/EN, parent |
| `MateriaPrima` / `Ingrediente` (Raw material / Ingredient) | Supply-chain input | name, origin, seasonality |
| `Mercato` (Market) | Recurring district market/fair | periodicity, place, days |
| `Evento` (Event) | One-off commercial event | date, place, theme |
| `Quartiere` / `ZonaCommerciale` (Neighborhood / Commercial zone) | Geographic area | name, boundaries/center, commercial street |
| `Certificazione` / `Riconoscimento` (Certification / Recognition) | Quality mark | type (PDO, PGI, historic shop…), body |
| `Cliente` / `Utente` (Customer / User) | Consumer profile (privacy-protected) | preferences, area, opt-in history |
| `Recensione` (Review) | Evaluative contribution | score, text, author, date |
| `Tradizione` / `Tema` (Tradition / Theme) | Narrative thread (e.g. "local ceramics") | description, period |

### 5.2 Relationship types (edges)

| Relationship | From → To | Meaning |
|--------------|-----------|---------|
| `VENDE` (SELLS) | Shop → Product/Service | The shop offers the product |
| `PRODOTTO_DA` (PRODUCED_BY) | Product → Artisan/Producer | Who makes it |
| `USA_MATERIA_PRIMA` (USES_RAW_MATERIAL) | Product → RawMaterial | Supply chain / ingredients |
| `FORNISCE` (SUPPLIES) | Producer → Shop | Supply relationship (short supply chain) |
| `APPARTIENE_A_CATEGORIA` (BELONGS_TO_CATEGORY) | Product/Shop → MerchandiseCategory | Classification |
| `SITUATO_IN` (LOCATED_IN) | Shop/Market → Neighborhood | Localization |
| `VICINO_A` (NEAR) | Shop ↔ Shop | Geographic proximity |
| `COMPLEMENTARE_A` (COMPLEMENTARY_TO) | Shop/Product ↔ Shop/Product | Cross-selling / combined purchases |
| `PARTECIPA_A` (PARTICIPATES_IN) | Shop/Artisan → Market/Event | Presence at markets/events |
| `HA_CERTIFICAZIONE` (HAS_CERTIFICATION) | Shop/Product → Certification | Recognized quality |
| `RECENSISCE` (REVIEWS) | Customer → Shop/Product (via Review) | Feedback |
| `PREFERISCE` / `HA_ACQUISTATO` (PREFERS / HAS_PURCHASED) | Customer → Shop/Product | Personalization (opt-in) |
| `INCARNA_TRADIZIONE` (EMBODIES_TRADITION) | Shop/Product → Tradition/Theme | Territorial storytelling |
| `SIMILE_A` (SIMILAR_TO) | Product ↔ Product | Semantic similarity (from embedding) |

### 5.3 Edge weighting criteria

The weight (normalized value, e.g. 0–1) makes the graph *navigable with priority*. It is **derived and recalculated**, never inserted by hand arbitrarily, from factors configurable per relationship type:

| Factor | Applies to (examples) | Weighting logic |
|--------|-----------------------|-----------------|
| **Geographic proximity** | `VICINO_A`, `SITUATO_IN` | Weight decreasing with distance (closer = stronger) |
| **Frequency/co-occurrence** | `COMPLEMENTARE_A`, `HA_ACQUISTATO` | The more often two nodes are connected/co-purchased, the higher the weight |
| **Community quality** | `RECENSISCE`, Shop/Product ranking | Average votes + count + recency; increases weight and visibility |
| **Source reliability** | all | Verified/claimed retailer > connector > anonymous community; modulates the weight |
| **Verification status** | nodes and edges | `verified`/`claimed` weighs more than `draft` |
| **Recency / freshness** | hours, availability, events | Recent data weighs more than old data (temporal decay) |
| **Supply-chain strength** | `USA_MATERIA_PRIMA`, `FORNISCE` | Short supply chain/zero-km and bilateral declaration increase the weight |
| **Semantic relevance** | `SIMILE_A`, query match | Cosine similarity from Qdrant between descriptions/intent |
| **Seasonality** | `VENDE`, `Prodotto` | Boost of in-season products in the current period |
| **AI usage feedback** | all | Edges/nodes that led to useful answers (clicks, saves) are reinforced |

The final weight of an edge is a combination (configurable per domain) of these factors. The weighting function is immutable in the sense of the project style: every recalculation produces a new versioned value, the audit preserves the history.

## 6. Data sources & connectors (ingestion)

Ingestion reuses the existing pipeline (Spring Batch, Tika/OCR extraction, chunking, embedding, Qdrant) and the PF4J plugin system for the domain-specific connectors.

| Source | Connector type | What it extracts | Mode |
|--------|----------------|------------------|------|
| Manual UI entry | Native | All entities | On-demand |
| CSV/JSON import (municipal lists, chambers of commerce) | Bulk import | Retailer registries, categories | Batch on-demand |
| Territorial open data (municipalities, regions, public markets) | Plugin connector | Markets, events, public registries | Scheduled |
| Public business listings / maps | Plugin connector | Hours, contacts, public reviews, geo | Scheduled (respecting ToS/robots) |
| Retailer's management system / e-commerce (CSV, API, feed) | Per-retailer plugin connector | Product catalog, prices, availability | Scheduled/webhook |
| Flyers, price lists, menus (PDF/images) | Document pipeline (Tika + Tesseract OCR) | Products, prices → extraction + AI structuring | Batch |
| Social / event pages | Plugin connector | Events, markets, promotions | Scheduled |
| Email (offers, supplier price lists) | `email` domain (IMAP) | Price lists, availability | Scheduled |
| Community contributions | Native | Reviews, photos, reports, votes | Event-driven |

Each connector: produces candidate nodes/edges, passes through validation/deduplication (§3.5), records provenance and timestamp, respects the legal/ToS constraints of the sources, and works **without sending data to external services** (extraction and structuring via local Ollama AI by default).

## 8. AI / GraphRAG use cases

Examples of real queries that GraphRAG must be able to satisfy by combining structural filters, semantics, and navigation of the weighted graph:

1. **Contextual proximity discovery:** "Where do I buy naturally leavened bread open now within 600 m?" → geo filter + hours + semantic intent + quality ranking.
2. **Transparent supply chain:** "Which shops use flours from the town's mill?" → traversal `VENDE`→`USA_MATERIA_PRIMA`→`MateriaPrima`(local mill).
3. **Intelligent composition:** "Create a gift basket of only neighborhood specialties under 40 €" → selection of `Prodotto` by category/price/proximity + complementarity.
4. **Rare repairs/services:** "Who repairs leather boots downtown?" → service category + artisan expertise + proximity.
5. **Cross-selling for retailers:** "Which businesses are complementary to mine for a joint campaign?" → weighted `COMPLEMENTARE_A` edges + supply chain.
6. **Shopping itinerary:** "An afternoon of local shopping on foot between crafts and gastronomy" → path on the graph by neighborhood/theme.
7. **Non-obvious connections:** "Show hidden connections between cheese producers and the territory's restaurants" → suggestion of missing edges.
8. **Seasonality:** "What local specialty is in season now and where do I find it?" → seasonal boost + availability.
9. **Q&A with citations:** every answer cites the nodes/paths used and the criteria (proximity, quality, supply chain), as required by the core GraphRAG.

In all cases the local AI (Ollama) is the default; cloud providers remain optional and are never used with sensitive commercial data without explicit consent.

## 10. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| Sparse/incomplete data in poorly covered territories | Weak AI responses | Connectors + bulk import + community contributions; graceful degradation of the response |
| Quality/reliability of external data | Wrong/duplicate nodes | Fail-fast validation, deduplication, verification status, tracked provenance |
| Slow retailer adoption | Unclaimed storefronts | Low-friction onboarding (claim, extraction from flyers), value shown via analytics |
| Spam/fake reviews | Distorted ranking | Moderation (rules + local AI), weighting by source reliability, audit trail |
| Legal/ToS constraints of sources | Compliance risk | Connectors that respect robots/ToS, preference for open data, explicit consent |
| Privacy of commercial and customer data | Compromised trust | Local-first, local AI by default, no cloud sending without consent, sensitive data not public |
| Graph query scalability on MySQL+Qdrant (no Neo4j) | Latency on large graphs | Targeted indexes, depth-limited queries, caching (Caffeine), pre-calculation of weights/neighbors |
| Ranking bias toward the most active | Penalized retailers | Balance recency/novelty, "fairness boost" to new verified nodes |
| Taxonomy/enum IT/EN maintenance | Data drift | Review process, consistency tests for bilingual enums |

## 11. Maintenance & evolution

- **Evolving schema:** new node/relationship types are added via modular schema and Flyway migrations (one query per file); enums stay synchronized IT/EN toward the frontend.
- **Continuous calibration of weight/ranking:** periodic review of the parameters based on the KPIs (§9) and AI usage feedback; every change versioned.
- **Connectors as PF4J plugins:** independent lifecycle, updatable without touching the core; publishable on the marketplace.
- **AI model update:** GraphRAG prompts and embedding models revised with the new versions of Spring AI/Ollama; response-quality evaluations.
- **Data quality:** periodic deduplication jobs, freshness verification (hours/availability), source reconciliation.
- **Bilingual documentation:** keep the IT (`documentazione/`) and EN (`documentation/`) versions aligned; update the `Sviluppi/` folder at each intervention, per the project rules.
- **Domain roadmap:** from the consumer MVP toward hybrid scenarios (tourism + commerce + events) on the same graph, and toward district/consortium packages installable from the marketplace.

| `llm` (`LlmGatewayService`) | Answer generation, structuring, embedding | Ollama default; fallback chain; catalog embedding |
| `document` + batch (Tika/OCR) | Extraction from flyers/menus/price lists | Ingestion pipeline → Product nodes |
| Qdrant (`vectorstore`) | Semantic similarity of products/intent | Embedding and search for `SIMILE_A` and query match |
| MySQL + Flyway | Graph structure (nodes/edges/weights) and registries | New tables/migrations (one query per file), UUID `CHAR(36)` |
| `plugin` (PF4J) + `marketplace` | Installable connectors and domain packages | Open data/management-system connectors; commerce module from the marketplace |
| `email` | Ingestion of supplier price lists/offers | IMAP connector → Product/availability nodes |
| `calendar` | Recurring markets and events | Synchronization of events/markets ↔ Event/Market nodes |
| `automation` | Scheduled ingestion/weight-recalculation jobs | Periodic triggers for connectors and ranking |
| `messaging` / `channels` | Notifications to retailers/customers, contact | Alerts on discoveries, claims, events |
| `agent` | AI agents for onboarding/assisted curation | Extraction, deduplication, assisted moderation |
| `auth` | Identity of retailers/curators/customers | Roles and permissions (claim, moderation) |
| `common` (domain events, analytics) | `CommerceNodeUpdated` events, metrics | Re-embedding, weight recalculation, KPIs §9 |
| Angular frontend (Signals, i18n) | UI for storefronts, search, interactive graph, retailer management | New lazy-loaded `commerce` feature, Signal store, IT/EN language switch |

The domain **introduces no new infrastructure** (no Neo4j): it reuses MySQL + Qdrant, the hexagonal architecture (pure domain wired in `DomainConfig`), the plugin system, and the existing pipeline — consistently with the project constraints (local-first, local AI by default, privacy, open source, bilingual IT/EN).
